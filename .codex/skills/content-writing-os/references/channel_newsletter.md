# Newsletter Channel Guide

## Purpose

Write long-form, decision-grade explanations for non-technical business owners/operators:
deep enough to trust, simple enough to repeat.

This guide is based on the published “Codex Introduction” series format in this repo.

## Style

- Teach from first principles.
- Separate facts, interpretation, and recommendations.
- Use a recurring concrete workflow moment early (story beat), then generalize.
- Prefer clarity over cleverness. Use headings for navigation and scanning.

## Tone

- Calm, practical, direct.
- Technical but clear.
- No inflated claims; name limits and failure modes.

## Format Rules

- Target length: 2200–3400 words (including checklists + evidence appendix).
- Include 3–5 subject line options at the top.
- Use the canonical section set below (keep the exact heading names for consistency).
- Use short paragraphs (2–4 sentences).
- Use numbered steps when teaching a workflow; bullets for lists.
- No em dashes (—). Use commas or separate sentences.

### Canonical Sections (required)

1. `Subject Line Options`
2. `{{Issue Title}}` (H1)
3. `Subheading`
4. `Hook`
5. `Introduction`
6. `This Issue's Insight`
7. `Technical Concept Explained`
8. `Why This Is Useful For The Business`
9. `What It Means In Practice`
10. `Screenshot Plan`
11. `Action Checklist`
12. `CTA`
13. `Evidence Map`
14. `Searchable Key Phrases` (exactly 5)

## QA Checklist (before publishing)

- Does the reader get the plain-English definition before mechanics?
- Is there at least one concrete failure mode explained?
- Is every factual claim mapped in `Evidence Map` (or rewritten as hypothesis)?
- Does each major section end with a decision implication or action?
- Are the section headers consistent with the canonical set?
